## 3. Instability — the bigger prize

Averages understate the risk. Ranking by worst observed run against average
([`02_instability.csv`](../reports/02_instability.csv)):

| Job | Runs | Avg | P95 | Max | Max ÷ Avg | Worst-night excess |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `JCP1484U` | 32 | 27.45 | 24.83 | **319.87** | 11.7× | +292 min |
| `JCPAAS09` | 32 | 10.11 | 7.85 | **145.57** | 14.4× | +135 min |
| `JCPU516` | 33 | 12.73 | 18.77 | 36.98 | 2.9× | +24 min |
| `JAIMBLS` | 32 | 9.41 | 24.97 | 26.08 | 2.8× | +17 min |
| `JCPGL001` | 32 | 3.12 | 2.92 | 20.25 | 6.5× | +17 min |

`JCP1484U` ran for **5 hours 20 minutes** on its worst night against a 27-minute average, and
its P95 (24.83) sits *below* its average — the signature of one or two catastrophic runs rather
than gradual drift. On a normal night it is the 13th largest job; on a bad night it is larger
than the entire A7#2 chain. `JCPAAS09` has the same shape.

Across the 391 jobs with ≥20 runs and ≥1 minute average, **687 minutes per cycle** separates
P95 from average. That is the swing operations must budget for, and it is the single largest
recoverable block of batch window in the estate.

**Two runaway jobs are worth more than any parameter tuning in this document.** Pull the job
logs for the outlier dates on `JCP1484U` and `JCPAAS09` before anything else.

## 4. Tuning surface found in the JCL

From the captured JCL evidence (731 of 2,475 MY members — see §7):

| Signal | Members | Report |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Running SORT / ICETOOL | 344 | — |
| …with **no** `DYNALL*` control member | **287** | [`05`](../reports/05_sort_dynalloc_candidates.csv) |
| Landing sort output on permanent DASD | 60 | [`06`](../reports/06_sort_intermediates.csv) |
| Still running `IEBGENER` | 2 | [`07`](../reports/07_copy_candidates.csv) |
| Running `IDCAMS` | 388 | — |

Cross-referencing those signals against measured runtime gives **212 members carrying
353 minutes of measured elapsed time per cycle** that have at least one JCL-level tuning
signal — [`09_priority_targets.csv`](../reports/09_priority_targets.csv).

### 4.1 Sort work allocation — 287 members

Only 57 captured members reference a `DYNALL*` control member; the shop already has `DYNALL32`,
so the pattern is established and simply not applied consistently. The rest run on whatever
`SORTWKnn` DD cards the member happens to code, which is why sort capacity failures track
file growth.

Fix: add one DD to the step and change nothing else.

```jcl
//SORTCNTL DD  DSN=&CNTLLIB(DYNALL64),DISP=SHR
```

[`../jcl/DYNALL64.ctl`](../jcl/DYNALL64.ctl) supplies `DYNALLOC=(SYSDA,64)`, `MAINSIZE=MAX`,
`HIPRMAX=OPTIMAL` and `DSA=128`. Every one of those is allocation or storage only — **none
changes record content or sort order**, so each is provable with `IEBCOMPR`.

The member deliberately excludes `NOEQUALS` and `VLSHRT`. Both are genuine performance options
and both change results; they are per-job decisions for the application owner, never a
shop-wide default.

Highest-value candidates with measured runtime: `JCP1028U` (46.12 min, the largest tunable
job in plan TMYCLKA7),
`JCP1056U` (11.03), `JCP0459R` (9.08, also writes 3 permanent intermediates).

### 4.2 Sort intermediates on permanent DASD — 60 members

60 members write sort output to a permanent `.SORT` / `.SRT` / `.SORTED` dataset and read it
back in a later step. Each intermediate costs a full write plus a full read. The worst are
`JCP0756R` (10 intermediates, 49 datasets), `JCP0540R` (6), `JCP0214R` (4), `JCP0363R` (4),
`JCP0848U` (4), `JCP1233U` (4).

Fix: collapse the fan-out into a single DFSORT pass with `OUTFIL FNAMES=` — pattern **P3** in
[`../jcl/PATTERNS.jcl`](../jcl/PATTERNS.jcl). Keep the output DSNs if downstream jobs or the
rerun procedure reference them; only the extra pass disappears.

### 4.3 VSAM buffering

The CP6 mains read the masters (`CPBCRD`, `CPBCUS`, `CPBPLT`, `CPBKYC`, `KCMACC`, `EPTRANS`)
through CP3 I/O modules end to end. Sequential VSAM with default buffers is the classic
Cardlink elapsed-time sink, and it is fixed in JCL without touching a line of COBOL:

```jcl
//CPBCRD   DD  DSN=&ENV.CIS.BTHVSM.&VER.CPBCRD,DISP=SHR,
//             AMP=('ACCBIAS=SO')
```

Patterns **P1** (sequential, keyed and non-SMS variants), **P5** (large sequential extracts:
`BUFNO=64`, `BLKSIZE=0`, extended format) and **P7** (bulk `REPRO` → DFSORT copy) are in the
same file. All are performance-only.

### 4.4 Partitioning a long runner

For `JCP1488U` and `JCP2513U`, parameter tuning will not close a 70-minute gap. Pattern **P6**
splits the master by key range so the ranges run as parallel jobs in the same plan.

This is the only recommendation here that needs an OPC change and an application-owner
decision: it is safe **only** where the program carries no cross-account state and no control
total spanning the whole file. Confirm before use — and note that neither member appears in
the captured JCL evidence, so their step structure has not been read yet.

## 5. Expected effect, honestly bounded

No elapsed-time saving is claimed as a number here, and that is deliberate. The repository
gives job-level elapsed time, not step-level or CPU-versus-I/O splits, so the share of each
job spent in sort or in VSAM I/O is unknown until measured. Publishing a percentage would be
invention.

What the data does support:

1. **353 minutes per cycle** of measured elapsed time sits in members with a JCL-level tuning
   signal. That is the bounded opportunity for §4.1–4.3.
2. **687 minutes per cycle** of P95-over-average headroom is the stability prize in §3.
3. **72 minutes per cycle** of drift has accumulated since Dec-25 and is unexplained.

Measure, don't estimate: [`../jcl/JMYBTUNE.jcl`](../jcl/JMYBTUNE.jcl) runs the untuned and
tuned outputs through `ICETOOL COUNT` and `IEBCOMPR`, so each change is proved
output-identical and timed on the same data before it is proposed to CAB.

## 7. What this analysis does *not* cover

State these limitations in the CAB pack rather than letting a reviewer find them.

- **JCL evidence is partial.** 731 of the 2,475 MY JCL members were recoverable; both Google
  Drive renderings of the repository truncate at ~1 MB, at `JCP1346R` alphabetically. The four
  long poles (`JCP1488U`, `JCP2513U`, `JCP1704U`, `RMMDLM01`) fall past the cut, so §4's member
  counts are a **lower bound** and the long poles have not had their steps read. Re-running
  [`../analysis/extract_repository.py`](../analysis/extract_repository.py) against the full
  `.xlsx` on a workstation closes this gap and the rest of the analysis reruns unchanged.
- **Dependencies are partial.** 536 MY relationships are marked `Parsed`; continuation-only
  OPC lines were never recovered. Every chain in §1 is a **proven lower bound**, never an upper
  bound. The real critical path can only be longer.
- **No step-level or CPU data.** The repository records job elapsed time only. Step-level
  attribution needs SMF 30 records.
- **`JCL_Inventory_SG`, `Validation_Issues`, `Dashboard` and `SG_SLA` were not recovered** from
  either rendering. None affects the MY conclusions.
- **Nothing here has been executed.** The JCL artifacts are lint-clean against the IA Checker
  ([`08_ia_checker.md`](../reports/08_ia_checker.md)) and are syntactically valid, but no job
  has run on a mainframe from this repository.

Sections **H and I apply to every change.**

Section A items to satisfy:

- **A1** — convert the production members to the test version with the JCL Converter. The
  production form stays the master; the converted version is never promoted.
- **A2** — at least one successful end-to-end run. Run `JMYBTUNE` **twice**; second-run
  behaviour is where GDG and catalog defects surface.
- **A3** — `DYNALLOC=(SYSDA,64)` and the `SPACE` figures in the patterns are sized for
  production volumes, and all secondary allocations meet the 100-CYL shop minimum.
- **A5** — condition-code control is at step level, not job level.

Section I — deployment is a member copy, no compile and no genbase:

| Job | Direction | Contents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pre-deployment safety copy | `PRDCRD.BTHJCL.MY20A2` → `.FALLBACK` | Every member in the package |
| Deployment | `MGRCRD.*` → `PRDCRD.*` | Same member list |
| Fallback | `.FALLBACK` → `PRDCRD.BTHJCL.MY20A2` | Same member list |

The **three-way symmetry check** is the one that bites: the identical member list must appear
in all three jobs, plus `DYNALL64` in `PRDCRD.BTHCTL.MY20A2`. A member present in two of the
three is the defect to look for. Update the Version Tracker (I9) and register the RFC (I4).

Because no load module changes, fallback is a copy back of the JCL and CTL members — well
inside the ≤1 hour fallback commitment on the RFC risk assessment.
